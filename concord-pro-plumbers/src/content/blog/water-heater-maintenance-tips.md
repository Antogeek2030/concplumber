A little preventive care goes a long way toward avoiding emergency calls and costly replacements.

## Flush the Tank Annually

Sediment is the enemy of tank water heaters. Once a year, attach a hose to the drain valve and flush until the water runs clear. If you are not comfortable doing this yourself, schedule a professional maintenance visit.

## Check the Anode Rod

The sacrificial anode protects the steel tank from corrosion. In Concord’s water, many anodes last only 4–6 years. Replacing it can add years of service life to an otherwise healthy tank.

## Test the T&P Valve

The temperature and pressure relief valve is a critical safety device. Carefully lift the lever and let a small amount of water discharge into a bucket. If it doesn’t open or drips afterward, it needs replacement.

## Inspect for Leaks and Corrosion

Look at fittings, the drain valve, and the base of the unit every few months. Catching a slow leak early prevents floor damage.

## Keep the Area Clear

Maintain clearance around the water heater for ventilation (especially gas units) and easy service access. Never store flammable materials nearby.
